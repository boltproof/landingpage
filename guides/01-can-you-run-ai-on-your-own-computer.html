Can you really run AI on a computer you already own?

Yes. Here's why running a model is nothing like training one, and the three RAM tiers that decide what your machine can run.

Yes. This is the question everyone asks first, so let me answer it directly.

The version of AI you've heard about, the one with the data centers and the billions of dollars, is for training models. That's the expensive part, and you are not doing it. What you're doing, running a model, is a completely different and much cheaper activity.

A model is just a file. A big file full of numbers, but a file. When you run it, that file loads into your computer's memory, and it answers your questions. If your machine has enough memory for the file, it works. That is the entire technical requirement.

Here's what "enough memory" looks like, in plain numbers:

  • 8 GB runs the small models. Fine for basic writing and quick summaries.
  • 16 GB runs the mid-size models that do most business writing comfortably. This is the sweet spot.
  • 32 GB runs the noticeably smarter models. Worth it if the AI is doing real heavy lifting.

That's it. No graphics card needed. Modern local AI runs on a plain processor, just a bit slower. The graphics card makes it faster, but it's a bonus, not a requirement. Anyone who tells you otherwise is usually trying to sell you a graphics card.

The machine you already have probably qualifies. If your laptop or desktop has 16 GB of RAM, you're done. And if it doesn't, the best value play in this whole field is a used office PC for a few hundred dirhams here in Dubai. It'll sit in a cupboard running your AI quietly for years, offline, for the cost of electricity.

So the honest answer is: you probably don't need to buy anything. You need to install a free program and point it at a free model. There's a guide for that.
